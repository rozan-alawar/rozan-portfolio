Developer Profile

I build for the hardest conditions, not the demo

Hi, I'm Rozan Abu Alawar — a Flutter developer whose apps hold up under government review, weak networks, old devices, and users who can't see the screen.

Most of my work has been for people in genuinely difficult circumstances: a national ministry that can't ship a rendering error in scripture, displaced and blind users in Gaza who need aid services they can reach entirely by voice, listeners on connections that drop constantly.

That's shaped how I build. Accessibility isn't a checklist I run at the end, offline isn't an edge case, and I care more about how an app behaves on a four-year-old Android on 3G than how it looks in a screenshot. Four apps live on the App Store and Google Play.

Working Together

What it's actually like to work with me

Skill is easy to check. Whether someone is reliable is harder — so here's how I run a project.

You hear from me weekly

Day-to-day questions go through WhatsApp, so nothing waits days for an answer. On top of that you get a written update every week: what shipped, what's next, and anything that has become a risk. You should never have to ask how it's going.

The code is yours, and it's documented

You get the full repository along with a README, architecture notes and setup instructions — written so another developer could pick the project up without me. I build assuming I won't be the last person in the codebase.

I handle the store submissions

Builds, store listings, review responses and release — I do all of it, on your developer accounts so you keep ownership. App review rejections are handled as part of the job, not treated as a surprise. I've taken four apps through this on both stores.

I don't disappear at launch

Every project includes a bug-fix window after release — if something I built breaks in that period, I fix it, and it isn't a new negotiation. Launch is when real users arrive and real problems surface, which is exactly the wrong moment for your developer to vanish.

When requirements change mid-project — and they usually do — I'll tell you what it affects and what it costs in time before I start, so you can decide. No silent scope creep, and no surprise invoices.
